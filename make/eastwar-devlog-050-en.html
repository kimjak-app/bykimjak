Dev Log · #050

Adding Scenery to the 3D Battlefield

Soil, hills, trees and forests, added with Grok Coach

October 26, 2026 · Terrain appearance work with Grok Coach

I added the colors of soil and grass to the 3D battlefield I had built in Blender, along with hills, trees and forests. The battlefield's framework was already there; this time, the work was about refining the scenery visible on top of it. I put Grok Coach in charge of this part.

In the previous dev log, I moved the HUD from the 2D view to the 3D screen. With the interface for choosing commanders and checking their information in place, it was time to work on the ground behind it. I am gradually filling in how the cells beneath units, the river crossings and the scenery beyond the battlefield should look.

The deliverables included ground textures and code for rendering trees, river water and terrain beyond the battlefield. Grok Coach reported generating two images for each of 14 terrain types with Python, for a total of 28 PNG files. One is an albedo map containing the colors of soil or grass; the other is a normal map that makes surface contours show up under lighting.

Even with color added, the existing battlefield's heights and terrain classifications need to stay aligned. This work used heightfield.json, which records elevation, and terrain_types.json, which identifies ground types. The approach is to read the battlefield data already defined and align the visible ground and river with it.

The battlefield's edges were also part of the work. I received a report that the ground was arranged to continue beyond the grid rather than end in a cliff, with distant areas blending into grayish-yellow fog. The method connects boundary vertices to join the surfaces. It also included aligning the joins so that gaps would not appear where faces of different sizes meet.

Grok Coach said it calculated these seams in Python and found a height difference of 0.000000m across 480 vertices. It also calculated a layout of 72 trees across 24 forest cells, checking their distances from cell boundaries and the spacing between trees. Numbers like these help me understand what was checked and against which criteria.

These results, however, were verified in a computational model. They do not mean that every angle and zoom level was checked in the actual game view. Grok Coach's working environment did not include a workflow for running Godot 4.6 with GPU-rendered visuals and making adjustments by looking at them directly. Whether the colors are too muddy, the rocks too shiny or the ground boundaries naturally connected still needs to be judged separately in the running scene.

It also reported using a lightweight approach to the river. To suit the gl_compatibility environment, the setup represents water depth and foam using color information stored in mesh vertices, without reading a separate depth texture. I intend to check the method used and whether it actually looks good as separate questions. Having the code ready does not mean the water's color and reflections are already where I want them.

The trees still need refinement, too. These are models whose shapes were generated in code. Placing them lets me see where the forest will be, but the silhouettes formed by their branches and foliage leave room for improvement. In combat, I need to examine not just the trees themselves but also how clearly units behind them can be seen.

To protect the existing combat code, I kept these deliverables separate for inspection in a dedicated preview scene. It is set up so that elements such as trees and bridges can be toggled on and off for comparison. The feature that fades trees when a unit enters a forest is at the stage of having a callable interface. Connecting it to the main game so it works automatically with actual unit movement remains to be done.

For the next step, I received a proposal to make the terrain images used outside the battlefield more detailed. It calls for replacing the current 256×256 placeholder images with 2048×2048 images, and adjusting the colors and textures to flow naturally between plains, hills, rocks and riverbanks. Refining the tree silhouettes and translucency is another candidate. These are not completed changes; they are tasks whose priorities I will decide after looking at the scene.

Once the appearance is reasonably settled, it also needs to be connected to the combat scene. Trees need to be handled so they do not obscure units entering a forest, and features for marking movement cells or attack targets, as well as effects showing spreading fire or scorch marks, need to work together with the terrain.

This time, I added soil, hills, trees and forests to the battlefield already in place. I was able to receive Grok Coach's files and calculation results, and distinguish the parts that still need refinement in the running view. Next, I plan to continue by checking whether units are easy to see in this scenery and whether the places they can move are easy to recognize.

Next: Making Terrain Areas More Distinct

Next, I want to refine the terrain so that each cell's type is more clearly visible. According to Grok Coach, automatic material handling based on ground height and slope currently works alongside boundary smoothing, which can make the visible texture differ from the assigned terrain. I plan to adjust this so that a cell designated as grassland still reads as grassland at a higher elevation, and soil and mud appear where intended.

The reference is the per-cell terrain recorded in terrain_types.json. Grok Coach explained that the current 20×20 terrain_control_map stores the base terrain separately from overlaid states such as snow, mud and scorching. The next revision is intended to make the screen reflect these assigned values more faithfully.

For this, terrain_type_strictness is planned as a value controlling how strongly the assigned terrain is retained, while cell_blend_margin will control how widely materials mix at cell edges. The idea is to keep the original terrain's characteristics clear in the center of a cell and connect it to its surroundings only within a narrow band at the edges.

This feature has not been implemented yet. In the next round of work, I plan to add this distinction and check whether grassland and soil, forests and hills remain recognizable from a distance. I want the terrain's visual treatment to help players read what kind of cell they are looking at during combat, too.
