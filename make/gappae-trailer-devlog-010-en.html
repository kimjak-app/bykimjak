Gappae Trailer

Production Log

How the trailer gets made, from concept and shot design to the final cut, one shot at a time.

Production Log · #010

Starting Video Production

Taking the Blender Previs into Seedance 2.5 for Beat 4

October 1–5, 2026

In the previous log, I used Blender to establish the camera movement and arrow speed for Beat 4. This log records the first full production task using that video. Beat 1 was another candidate, but its method relied on fixed start and end stills. Beat 4 was the right example for showing how the Blender work continued into video generation.

October 1–5: 106 Generations, 60 Used in the Edit

Between October 1 and 5, 2026, I generated 106 videos with Seedance 2.5. 60 of those videos contributed footage to the final four-minute trailer. That does not mean all 60 were complete OK takes. Even when an entire take was unsuitable, I retained useful sections and used them through cut editing in DaVinci Resolve.

The decision was not simply whether to accept or discard a whole video. I looked for the moments that could serve the finished sequence and made as much use of them as possible. By recovering usable portions even from takes that were not fully OK, I minimized discarded footage while building the four-minute film. Beat 4 shows how the generation instructions were designed; the complete trailer also depended on the director's selection and editing of the material.

First, the Principles of This Work

Before looking at the prompt, there are three principles behind the production.

1. The director edits personally in DaVinci Resolve. AI produces shots as raw material, rather than a finished film. The director selects and joins the shots, and adjusts their speed, color, and sound in DaVinci Resolve.

2. No background music in generated footage. The trailer's music had already been prepared. The generation stage must not introduce any music. Sound effects are allowed: the bowstring, the arrow in flight, and its impact on the tree can be generated with the shot.

3. Most generations are around 5–10 seconds long. This suits an edit assembled from short shots and helps limit the errors that can arise in longer generations. The Beat 4 example is five seconds long.

I supplied five references to Seedance 2.5.

The Complete Prompt

REFERENCE ROLES:
<<<video_1>>>= camera path, timing, framing and character positions ONLY. It is an untextured grey blockout: do NOT copy its grey capsules, colors or simple shapes.
<<<image_1>>>= the archer (the King). Exact face, costume and bow.
<<<image_2>>>= the young prince standing at the far end of the path. Exact face and costume.
<<<image_3>>>= the forest path seen toward the prince, with the huge old oak at the end of the path on its right edge.
<<<image_4>>>= the same path seen toward the archer.

Live-action Korean Joseon-dynasty historical film, photorealistic, one continuous unbroken shot following the camera path of the reference video exactly. Dry, crisp, cold daylight in a pine and oak forest.

0.0–1.3s: Close 3/4 front view from the King's right side. He draws the bow at full tension, eyes cold and shadowed, bowstring hand at left of frame, bow and arrowhead at right. Very slow push-in.
1.3–2.2s: Extreme slow motion release. The string snaps forward, the bow trembles, the arrow leaves the string and drifts left to right across frame, wobbling. The camera pans with the arrow and falls in directly behind it.
2.2–3.0s: The arrow accelerates down the forest path. It grazes past the head of a stag standing on the path, missing it by a hand's width; the stag flinches. The arrow ignores the stag and flies on.
3.0–3.8s: The camera stops and punches in. Slow motion again: the arrow flies straight at the prince's face. He steps half a pace aside and leans his whole upper body away. The arrow passes about 15 cm from his cheek and shoulder without touching his skin, hair or clothing.
3.8–4.1s: Back to real speed. The arrow slams deep into the trunk of the old oak behind him with a heavy impact and quivers.
4.1–5.0s: The prince turns to look back at the quivering arrow. Hold.

Sound: bowstring release, arrow whistle, wind past the face, deep thud into wood, stag's sharp breath, forest ambience. No background music, no score, no melody — sound effects and ambience only. No dialogue.

Avoid: arrow touching or piercing the prince's clothing or body, extra arrows, bow snapping, cuts or scene changes, grey blockout look, text, subtitles, modern objects.

Understanding Each Part of the Prompt

This prompt is closer to a specification than a piece of storytelling. Its five parts have different jobs.

1. Reference Roles

First, I assigned a role to each reference. The crucial instruction for the Blender video was to use only the camera path, timing, framing, and character positions, without copying its grey capsules, colors, or simple shapes. This keeps the blockout's appearance out of the final image. Yi Gak and Yi Hyeon's sheets define the faces, costumes, and bow; the two location images define the forest. Movement comes from the video, appearance from the images.

2. Establishing the Look

One paragraph establishes a live-action film, one continuous shot, the reference video's exact camera path, and dry, cold daylight in a pine and oak forest. It fixes the texture, continuity, and lighting of the scene.

3. The Timeline

I divided the five seconds into six intervals, carrying over storyboard shots 5, 6, and 7 and the design established in Blender.

IntervalAction
0.0–1.3 sRight-side three-quarter close-up of Yi Gak at full draw, with cold, shadowed eyes and a very slow push-in: storyboard shot 5.
1.3–2.2 sExtreme slow-motion release. The wobbling arrow crosses left to right; the camera pans with it and falls in directly behind it. This is the transition into the arrow-following continuous take.
2.2–3.0 sThe arrow accelerates down the path, missing the stag's head by a hand's width. The stag flinches, but the arrow ignores it and continues. The deer is not the target.
3.0–3.8 sThe camera stops and punches in; slow motion returns. Yi Hyeon steps aside and leans away. The arrow passes about 15 cm from his cheek and shoulder, reinforcing the clearance established in Blender.
3.8–4.1 sReal speed returns. The arrow embeds itself deeply in the old tree and quivers: storyboard shot 7.
4.1–5.0 sYi Hyeon turns toward the quivering arrow. Hold: breathing room for the edit and for the scene's aftermath.

The timeline contains a slow–fast–slow–fast rhythm. I restated Blender's speed changes in words, so that the video and text reinforce the same intention from two directions.

4. Sound

I specified the bowstring release, arrow whistle, wind past the face, a deep impact in wood, the stag's sharp breath, and forest ambience. As established above, sound effects are generated within the video. Each has a role: tension, speed, close clearance, the weight of impact, surprise, and a sense of place.

Background music, any score or melody, and dialogue are prohibited. The music was prepared separately, and music mixed into generated footage would make it unusable for this edit. Explicit sound instructions also leave less room for unintended music or noises.

5. What to Avoid

  • An arrow touching or piercing Yi Hyeon's clothes or body. The tension depends on the narrow miss.
  • Multiple arrows. There must be only one.
  • A broken bow. This prevents a physical error at the release.
  • Cuts or scene changes. The shot must stay continuous.
  • The grey Blender blockout look. The reference's appearance must not leak into the result.
  • Text, subtitles, and modern objects. These are unwanted additions.

The Resulting Beat 4 Shot

This is the five-second Seedance 2.5 result made from the Blender previs, character sheets, location images, and prompt. It becomes one piece of material for the director's edit.

Grok Was Used Only for the Opening Intro

Grok AI was used during the test-video stage, but its footage felt visually inconsistent with the main production. I could not use those test shots in the main body of the trailer. Only the opening intro uses footage generated with Grok. The supplied file, “인트로 영상_마스터01” (Intro Video Master 01), is that Grok-generated intro.

Bringing It Together

Three elements share the work of this shot.

  • The Blender video defines movement: camera, speed, and positions.
  • Character sheets and location images define appearance: faces, clothes, and forest.
  • The prompt defines rules: sequence, sound, and exclusions.

I believe this division helped the shot come out as intended on the first attempt. In DaVinci Resolve, this five-second shot joins the other footage over the music prepared in advance.

Across the production, 106 Seedance 2.5 generations supplied 60 videos used in the edit. Those 60 include partially usable takes as well as fully OK shots. The final four-minute trailer came from selecting and joining useful moments, making the most of the generated material and minimizing waste.
