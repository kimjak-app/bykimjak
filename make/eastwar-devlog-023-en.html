EASTWAR Dev Log #023

Making Research Actually Change the Game — The Tech Tree, One Month in the Making

July 8, 2026

Now that the enemy had started acting with real intent, the player needed a growth axis to respond with. That was the tech tree. And this project ran far longer than I expected — nearly a month, with almost 40 version tags attached to it.

Laying Down the Skeleton

The first step was moving the techs that only existed on a design document into actual data. City techs were sorted into agriculture, fishing, commerce, and military (infantry, archery, cavalry, navy, defense, siege); national techs into administration, economy, military, and diplomacy. At this point, none of it was visible yet — just a skeleton filled into a registry.

Making It Visible — and Clickable

Next was putting that skeleton on screen. The left side became the national tech tree, the right side the city tech tree — carrying over the world map's existing logic, where the left represents the whole nation and the right represents whichever city is currently selected. At first it was read-only. Clicking a node did nothing; only completed, available, and locked states were distinguished by color.

This is where the detail work began in earnest. I turned the big card-style graph into something more compact, and made it so clicking an icon only refreshed the detail panel. It sounds trivial, but at first, clicking a single node was rebuilding the entire graph — meaning the screen briefly stuttered on every click. Fixing that made click response noticeably snappier, and I bumped icon size from 56px up to 64px. That, in turn, brought back node overlap issues, especially in the city tech tree's military category. Instead of manually nudging each category, I fixed the spacing calculation globally.

Research Starts to Actually Move

Only once the UI had settled did I get into the real substance: making the "start research" button actually do something. First, I let research be started at all. Then I made remaining turns count down as turns passed. Finally, completion processing. Only after these three pieces were in place did the tech tree have its minimum viable skeleton — start research, progress through turns, complete.

Connecting the Five Categories, One at a Time

From here I moved carefully. Instead of wiring everything up at once, I attached categories one by one: economic effects first (agriculture, fishing, and commerce granting food/gold bonuses on completion), then military/defense effects (barracks, walls, training), national policy effects (taxation, bureaucracy, centralization), naval/siege display effects (turtle ships, cannons, siege weapons), and finally diplomacy/espionage display effects. At every step I asked the same questions: does this bonus apply to exactly that city, does it leak to other cities or the enemy, is there no bonus while research is still in progress. Once all five categories were connected, that was genuinely the finish line for the tech tree's first-pass implementation.

Actually Collecting the Cost

Once effects were wired in, cost came next. At first it was display-only — showing an estimated cost without actually deducting any resources. Then I made it so starting a research would deduct gold and food exactly once, with no per-turn drain and nothing extra taken on completion. Labor and policy — two more resource types — were designed but not implemented; I locked down the storage structure this round and left the actual implementation for later. The direction — accumulate over time, then spend it all on one big research push — was decided, but that part stays on the roadmap.

Checking It Myself in F6

Once all the numbers and logic were wired in, someone had to actually click through it. I added a grace period blocking invasions entirely for the first 10 turns, then ran through full scenarios starting and completing both national and city research from scratch. Does the early game have that light "let's pick one" feel? Does the mid-game make you weigh economy against military? Does the late game feel like it's building toward something? That kind of feel can't be verified by code — it took actually playing it and marking it PASS by hand.

Domestic Tech Complete Lock

After all of that, on July 7th, I declared the tech tree's first-pass implementation complete. I wrapped up everything done so far and drew a line so the tech tree work wouldn't keep expanding indefinitely. Future expansion — like labor and policy resources — would become its own separate project.

One Last Layer: Completion Presentation

The day after the completion lock, I added one more thing. Instead of research completion just quietly flashing a done indicator, national and city research each got their own short video that plays first, followed by a completion card. Nothing — no icon, no text — appears until the video finishes. Only then does the research icon, name, and a specific effect like "Administrative efficiency +5%" show up on the card. At first, this video played far too large, covering nearly the whole screen, so I had to resize it to match the same dimensions and ratio as the battle result video.

At the end of nearly a month of work, the tech tree stopped being just "a screen you can click" and became a system that actually changes a city's income, a nation's taxes, and what becomes available to unlock.
