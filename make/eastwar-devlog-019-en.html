EASTWAR Devlog #019

From Public Support to Espionage — A Nation Simulation Complete

May 30, 2026

Putting a Real Nation on Top of the Map

Last time, the world map and battle became one closed loop. This time was about putting a "living nation" on top of it. Not just war — feeding the people, collecting taxes, researching technology, talking to other nations, gathering secret intelligence. Most of what makes a real strategy game tick showed up this round.

Public Support and Loyalty — Two Separate Axes

Until now, there was only loyalty. This time I added a second axis: public support. Keeping the two clearly separate mattered. Public support reflects how well the people are actually living; loyalty reflects how much they submit to the state. Neither shifts turn by turn — instead, every season, high public support nudges loyalty up a little, and low support nudges it down. Public support is the cause; loyalty slowly follows as the effect.

Moving Troops Now Has a Cost

Previously, moving 100 troops to another city meant 100 troops simply arrived. Not anymore. If the origin city's loyalty is low, some troops desert or fall behind along the way. At loyalty 100, all 100 arrive; at loyalty 50, only half arrive and the rest vanish en route. Loyalty is no longer just a number — it now actually shapes military logistics.

Conscription, Recruitment, and a Growing Military

Cities with higher loyalty can now recruit more new soldiers. Low loyalty caps recruitment at 5%, while maxed-out loyalty pushes it up to 50%. Food factors in too — rice is consumed first, then barley, then seafood once those run out, meaning a city that's burned through its rice and barley in winter can still recruit using seafood, a real strategic fallback.

Revolt Doesn't Come Out of Nowhere

When public support and loyalty both bottom out, a city now sends up a warning sign — classified as stable, warning, or danger. Actual revolts and cities flipping hands aren't built yet; for now it's just the early warning. Losing a city without any notice felt far less fair than seeing "this city is at risk" and getting a chance to respond.

Technology Grows a Nation

I split the data for national-level technology and city-level technology. Techs now lock into each other — unifying currency unlocks the mint, and completing military reform unlocks upper-tier units like heavy infantry and the turtle ship. The turtle ship in particular has a hard-coded requirement: "must have Yi Sun-sin." A ship that can't exist without him — no exceptions there, period.

A Market Price Board Now Exists

Trade used to be just fixed route income. Now each resource has a base price that shifts with the season and circumstances. There's no buy/sell trade UI yet, but this lays the groundwork for special-goods trading and diplomatic trade agreements later.

Diplomacy — A Way Forward Besides War

Relations with other factions are now a score (0–100) plus a status: allied, neutral, hostile, or suspended. The first diplomatic action to go in was tribute — spending gold and silk to raise the relation score. Alliance proposals and declarations of war are still pushed to a later stage.

Espionage — The Invisible Battlefield

Espionage now requires a chancellor with high political skill. It started with quietly gathering intel on enemy cities, then extended to actively disrupting public support. Higher political aptitude means a bigger effect, but if discovered, relations take a serious hit and the action fails outright. There's now a way to shake up the board without ever declaring war.

What's Next

Public support → loyalty → conscription → revolt. Tech → trade. Diplomacy → espionage. Most of the axes that run a nation and shift the board — beyond just war — now exist. Next up is putting a real, hands-on UI on top of all this logic.
