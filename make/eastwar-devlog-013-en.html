Dev Log · #013

Three Days That Turned a Demo Into a Game

Move, face, fight, and architect — v0.64k ~ v0.65h

May 18 – May 20, 2026

DAY 1 · May 18 — The Game Loop Was Born

The first of the three days was the most dramatic. Until the day before, movement and combat were running as a rough demo — disconnected, incomplete. Then we expanded to fullscreen and re-aligned the 2D coordinates, and everything snapped into place at once. Unit movement, attack, defense, attack range — all fixed in one go.

And for the first time, this flow existed:

Select Yi Sun-sin → Move → Attack or Wait → Enemy turn → Guan Yu attacks → Return to ally turn → Move again

The day before, it was a movement and attack demo. From this day, it was the basic skeleton of a turn-based battle engine. The game loop was born.

Unit facing was also solved the same day. If the enemy is to the right, the troop body faces right. If left, left. Hero portraits, banners, and HP bars never flip — only the troop token image gets the flip_h treatment. The first moment two armies genuinely faced each other.

Then came the post-move facing selection UI. Move → facing panel appears → choose left/right/up/down → facing saved → attack or wait. It started as text buttons, then quickly upgraded to directional arrows embedded in the adjacent grid cells around the unit.

Enemy AI also got a major upgrade. Guan Yu stopped blindly chasing Yi Sun-sin and started running a Smart Move + Attack AI — BFS scanning all reachable cells, prioritizing positions where he can attack after moving. The feeling of an enemy that thinks, "if I cut this way, I can reach him," completely changed the feel of combat. That's what makes a tactical game.

One day. Cell blocking, rear attack, enemy AI movement, attack target selection mode, action lock after acting, right-click cancel and rollback. In terms of scope, it was practically an entire battle engine in a single session.

DAY 2 · May 19 — Units Got Real Faces

Day two was about visuals. The placeholder units finally started wearing actual Korean, Chinese, and Japanese troop images.

The infantry visual group was split into FootmanVisual.tscn. Unit body in the scene template, combat behavior in code, placement position in the Godot 2D editor — this role separation began here. Chinese cavalry art was connected to Guan Yu, and 2 ally / 2 enemy units were deployed together for the first time: Yi Sun-sin and Jeong Do-jeon vs. Guan Yu and Zhang Fei.

The attack UX also changed fundamentally. Auto-targeting was replaced by: press Attack → attack range highlights → click the enemy you want. The real value of this structure comes later. Hero abilities with area ranges, AoE attacks, terrain effects, buff/debuff targeting for strategic heroes like Jeong Do-jeon — all of it becomes possible on top of this foundation.

Multi-enemy AI was completed on day two as well. Zhang Fei started acting as an AI actor alongside Guan Yu. A golden Ready Frame now pulses on units that can still act, and spent units are visually distinguished automatically.

DAY 3 · May 20 — This Code Needs to Handle a Turtle Ship

The third day looked quieter on the surface. A round transition toast with an ink-spread shader reveal was added. Basic battle FX — movement dust, attack slash, hit spark, floating damage numbers — were bundled into a single FX pack.

But the real work was something else: the UnitVisualRoot refactor.

The v0.65g series moved the visual nodes of all four units — Yi Sun-sin, Jeong Do-jeon, Guan Yu, Zhang Fei — into their own VisualRoot containers, step by step. And partway through, an important principle was established:

AllyUnitVisualRoot is not Yi Sun-sin's slot.
The four Roots are test slots, nothing more.
In the final structure, any unit should be assignable to any slot via slot_id.

The structure being built now has to be able to receive Mongol cavalry, Turtle Ships, and Panokseon warships later. Infantry occupy a 1×1 cell. A Turtle Ship might be 2×2. Movement FX for land units is dust; for naval units, it should be a water wake. Miss this now, and the whole thing breaks later.

So in the v0.65h series, BattleUnitState received new meta fields: slot_id, nation, domain, footprint, visual_key, portrait_key, attack_fx_profile, move_fx_profile. These aren't fields that do anything yet. They're the plumbing laid now so that when naval combat and siege units arrive, the engine doesn't have to be rebuilt from scratch.

At the end of three days, the battle screen looked like this: four units deployed, each with a facing direction, turns exchanging, AI calculating positions, attacks triggering effects, hero panels alive and reading. And the structure underneath was designed to receive dozens more units down the line. The demo became a game.
