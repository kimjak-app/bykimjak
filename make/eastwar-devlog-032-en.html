Dev Log · #032

Banners Now Fly Over the Roster

Frames, Banners, and Battle Logs — Battle UI Polish Keeps Building

August 8, 2026

Rebuilding the Roster Header First

The last post covered the first styling pass on the central HUD and roster window. This time, the work moved into the actual data-driven roster components — AllyRosterHud and EnemyRosterHud — restructuring them from the inside. A new header structure got added to the top of the roster, and the existing 5 general cards got repositioned below it.

One Solid Frame to Wrap It All

The spec for the full roster panel frame got locked: 270×524px, transparent PNG.

This production-ready frame got test-placed as the bottom-most background for both the ally and enemy rosters, and the old blue/maroon header background plates got removed. The HeaderIcon and HeaderLabel, though, stayed as independent UI nodes — splitting the structure so the frame handles the visual background while header elements can still move independently.

Laying down the new frame surfaced an unexpected issue: the border highlight that used to mark the currently acting general disappeared. The cause needed identifying first — was the new frame just burying the border contrast, or had the original selection-style binding actually broken? Rather than inventing a new effect, the fix restored the original "highlight the acting general" contract that had existed before.

Raising Banners Beside the Roster

East Asian-style vertical war banners got test-placed as independent images on the battlefield-facing side of both the ally and enemy rosters.

The yellow square that used to sit in the HeaderIcon got removed, while the actual text — "Ally Formation" / "Enemy Formation" — stayed as-is. A small change, but this was the point where the roster stopped feeling like a plain info panel and started feeling like part of the battlefield.

Folding Down to Just the Portrait

An experiment ran alongside this to let the roster switch between two modes. Detailed-info mode and battlefield-focus mode each got wired to an independent button on the ally and enemy banners: expanded, everything shows exactly as it does now — frame, text, troops, unit type; collapsed, all of that disappears and only the general's portrait remains. In collapsed mode, portraits scale up roughly 15-20% to preserve presence, the selected general keeps its yellow border, and only dead or withdrawn generals dim. Ally portraits pull in tighter to the screen's left edge and enemy portraits to the right, with the banners moving along to match the collapsed-mode position. The collapse/expand transition runs on a short 0.3-second tween. This experiment was purely about presentation — actual battle logic and Battle_Land.tscn itself stayed untouched.

Resetting the Bottom of the Screen

After the roster came the bottom of the screen. Before making the battle log panel actually functional, the first step was simply placing a newly made square background frame into the test scene. This stage only built the "add the frame + make its position adjustable" structure — nothing more.

That work led to the finished form of the battle log panel.

Clearing the Bottom, Lightening the Turn Transition

The bottom of the screen used to have a long InteractionGuideHud guide bar. That bar got removed entirely in favor of a notification effect where "Ally Turn / Enemy Turn" text grows and shrinks when the turn switches. The first attempt paired that text with a large central banner display, but it made the screen look cluttered, so the direction changed.

The final approach reused the ally/enemy banners already standing beside the roster. When a faction's turn begins, that banner — right where it already sits — scales up to about 1.5x and eases back down in a short pulse. Rather than adding a new UI element, the choice was to convey the information through something already on screen.

What's Next

With the roster frame, banners, and battle log panel in place, the skeleton of the bottom UI is largely set. What's left is finishing the roster's collapse/expand mode for real, and wiring the turn-transition pulse effect into actual battle play.
