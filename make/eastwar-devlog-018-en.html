EASTWAR Devlog #018

From World Map to Battle, Fully Connected

May 29, 2026

Cities on the Map Start Coming Alive

Last time, the map existed and cities had taken their place on it. These two days were about making those cities actually do something. Click one and info shows up, turns cycle, enemies invade, battles happen, and the results flow back to the map — this was the first time the full loop came together.

Clicking a City Now Shows Something

Clicking a city marker now brings up an info panel. Same principle as always — don't build new, port what already exists. I pulled the city info window, chancellor/governor portraits, and policy UI straight from the web version and scaled it down for Godot. I also cleaned up the national warehouse display — instead of a wall of text like "rice 300 / salt 120 / gold 500," each resource now gets its own card showing current value, max value, and status (stable/short).

Turns Start Cycling

Ending my turn now passes to the enemy turn, then back to mine — sounds obvious, but this is the first time it actually existed. Turn count, month, and season update, and the screen refreshes. Actual enemy invasions weren't in yet at this point — getting the cycle itself locked down came first.

Enemies Actually Invade Now

Once the turn cycle was closed, real invasions went in. Ending my turn now rolls a chance for the enemy to invade. If one of their cities borders mine, they target it. Instead of jumping straight into battle, a card appears first — "Enemy forces are about to invade Hanseong" — letting me choose manual or automatic defense. Showing the threat and letting the player choose felt far more like a real game than a battle just starting without warning.

From World Map to Battle and Back Again

Now the hard part. Once the invasion choice is made, that information has to be packaged into something the battle engine understands — who's attacking, who's defending, which hero is bringing how many troops. All of it now survives the handoff into the battle screen. When the battle ends, the result (win/loss, troop losses) flows back to the world map and updates city ownership and troop counts. The world map and the battle engine — two worlds built completely separately — started talking to each other for the first time.

The Case of the Hero Who Appeared Out of Nowhere

Along the way, I caught a genuinely funny bug. Baekje invaded, and Liu Bei and Zhuge Liang — who belong to Chengdu and have nothing to do with this fight — showed up as reinforcements. At first it looked like a bug in how the world map picks reinforcement candidates. Digging deeper, the real cause was somewhere else entirely: whenever a battle slot was empty, an old "sample hero roster" left over from testing was auto-filling it. Finding the actual root cause meant I could fix it cleanly with one rule — battles coming from the world map only use the real roster, and empty slots just stay empty.

Getting Wounded, Captured, and Recovering

It felt wrong for a hero who lost a battle to just return as if nothing happened, so I added status. Wounded heroes fight weaker for a few turns before recovering. Captured heroes can't fight until they recover. It's not a deep rescue system yet — just the minimum rule: get hurt, get weaker; get captured, can't fight.

Now I Can Strike First Too

Until now, only the enemy could invade. This time I built the reverse — attacking an enemy city myself. Instead of diving straight into battle, there's now a "deployment prep" step: choosing which heroes to send, how many troops to allocate, and checking the food/gold/salt cost of the expedition.

Catching Up to the Web Version: 88%

Throughout all of this, I kept cross-checking against the web version — from how troops actually shrink, survive, and get wounded, to how battle results land on cities. By current count, it's about 88% parity with the original web version. It was 84% last check; the most recent commits pushed it further.

What's Next

Map, cities, turns, invasions, and battle are now one closed loop. Next time is about putting a real living nation on top of it — public support and loyalty, conscription and revolt, tech trees and diplomacy, espionage. The parts that can turn the tide without a single battle.
