Dev Log · #008

The Battlefield Lives

Goguryeo Rises, and the Battle Breathes

May 7 – May 8, 2026

Goguryeo Takes the Field

v0.3-5a ~ v0.3-5b — 2026.05.07

EASTWAR is a game where heroes from Korea, China, and Japan clash across East Asia. With Joseon's Yi Sun-sin and Jeong Do-jeon, Japan's Nobunaga and Kenshin, and Han's Guan Yu and Zhang Fei already in place — it was finally time for the third Korean faction to rise. Goguryeo, stationed in Pyongyang.

v0.3-5a placed Gwanggaeto the Great and Dorim in Pyongyang. Their designs were immediately interesting. Gwanggaeto's unique skill Yeongnak Grand Legacy raises all allied units' attack power by 20% for 2 turns — a classic commander's buff. Dorim's Black-White Scheming deals light damage to a selected enemy and a nearby one, while inflicting confusion and panic. Not a warrior — a strategist.

v0.3-5b brought their unique skills to life in actual combat, and added their portraits and cut-in images. When both skills fired in battle without a single console error — the third faction on the East Asian map was fully alive.

When the AI Refuses to Fight

v0.3-5c ~ v0.3-5e — 2026.05.07

There was a problem. Both Gwanggaeto and Dorim are buff and strategy-type heroes. The auto-battle AI, designed around straightforward attack logic, didn't know what to do with them. When they couldn't use their skills — or when a buff was already active — they'd simply stand still and wait. The battle wouldn't end.

The v0.3-5c~e patches rewrote the AI's decision tree. If a hero can't use their skill or has no valid target for it, they must now either advance toward an enemy or attempt a basic attack. Heroes suited for close combat close the distance; heroes with strong unique skills prioritize those first. A separate bug where Yi Sun-sin would repeatedly idle without attacking was also fixed here.

If the AI won't fight, there's no game. Obvious in theory — surprisingly hard to get right.

The Battle Starts to Speak — Status Icons and Cut-in Lines

v0.3-6a ~ v0.3-6i — 2026.05.07

Status effects like confusion, panic, and attack buffs had been building up inside the battle system — but players had no way to see them at a glance. v0.3-6a~d added status icons using symbols rather than images: confusion as 🌀, panic as ⚠, attack boost as ▲, defense boost as ◆. Burn, poison, stun, taunt, and bind were also pre-coded and waiting. Building the slots now, filling them later.

v0.3-6e~f strengthened the battle result presentation. Victory displays in gold, defeat in dark crimson — large text, no background box, just a subtle shadow, placed just above center on the battle panel. Skill names were also added to the cut-in display.

Then v0.3-6g~i — the real highlight. Battle lines. When a hero activates their unique skill, they say something.

When Gwanggaeto unleashes Yeongnak Grand Legacy: "My soldiers — trust me and follow!"

When Dorim activates Black-White Scheming: "My scheme — they will destroy each other!"

When Zhang Fei fires Changban Eruption: "Ha! One roar from me and they're all trembling!"

When voice acting and SFX arrive later, these lines will actually be heard. For now they're text — but the structure is already open for that day.

Laying the Foundation for 2.5D

v0.3-7a ~ v0.3-7f — 2026.05.07

Adding features and reinforcing structure are two different kinds of work. The v0.3-7 series was the latter.

v0.3-7a unified all grid-to-screen coordinate conversion through a single gridToScreen() function. The battlefield is flat now, but when it shifts to quarter-view or 2.5D projection later, only this one function needs to change — the entire screen follows. Battle logic, AI, and skills were untouched.

v0.3-7b separated the rendering into distinct layers for terrain, units, effects, and UI. v0.3-7c planted terrain data scaffolding for mountains, rivers, forests, bridges, and walls — not yet connected to battle rules, but the slots are there.

v0.3-7d~f reviewed the action presentation queue, improved damage number readability, and added a hit knockback reaction — units now briefly recoil when struck, then return to position. A small detail that makes the battlefield feel substantially more alive.

The battle screen being built now isn't a prototype to be torn down later. It's the skeleton that the final 2.5D will mount onto. Not "rebuild from scratch" — just "swap the adapter."

Choosing Who Fights — Hero Deployment

v0.4-0 ~ v0.4-0b — 2026.05.07

Until now, clicking the invasion button went straight to combat. v0.4-0 added one step in between: hero deployment selection. Click to invade, choose which garrisoned heroes to bring, then the battle begins. Even a single hero is enough to proceed.

It sounds like a small UI addition. But this one step is the foundation for future fatigue, injury, and garrison systems. Another bone in the skeleton, in place.

Turning Enemies Into Allies — Hero Recruitment

v0.4-1 ~ v0.4-2c — 2026.05.08

May 8th brought one of the most satisfying moments in building this game: enemy heroes joining your side after you take their city.

v0.4-1 implemented the MVP. Capture Luoyang, and Guan Yu and Zhang Fei transfer to the player's faction. They appear in the next battle's deployment roster. An enemy hero becomes your general — the core pleasure of a strategy game arrived for the first time.

v0.4-2 rebuilt the foundation underneath this system. Until now, city ownership, hero faction, and battle had been operating independently. Now they're linked: city ownership ↔ garrisoned heroes ↔ deployment roster ↔ hero transfer on capture. Heroes now have a locationCityId. The game tracks where each hero actually is.

v0.4-2a~b fixed a bug where Jeong Do-jeon's buff information was appearing on Gwanggaeto's info panel — solved by storing buff sources per hero rather than globally. v0.4-2c fixed left/right spawn positioning in battle: if the attacking city is to the right of the target, the attackers spawn on the right. The world map and battle screen now share a consistent sense of direction.

Moving Heroes, Glimpsing Management

v0.4-3 ~ v0.5-0a — 2026.05.08

v0.4-3 added hero redeployment. Heroes can now be freely moved between player-owned cities through a central modal. v0.4-3a added hero portrait icons to the HUD info panel for each city — glance at the map and see exactly who's where.

And then v0.5-0a — the domestic panel made its first appearance. A resource display panel now sits on the world map, showing each city's yields and figures as numbers and bars. No actual turn-based calculation yet. But the panel being there signals something: a game that was only about fighting has started thinking about management.

From a game about waging war — to a game about preparing for it. v0.5 has begun.
