Dev Log · #007

The Battle Grew Deeper

Effects, Tempo, and New Heroes

May 3 – May 6, 2026

Finding the Rhythm of Battle

v0.2-7o — 2026.05.03

At some point when you're building a game, you ask yourself: "Why does this battle feel so flat?" The units move, the numbers drop, the battle ends. Everything works — but something is missing. That something turned out to be rhythm.

In v0.2-7o, I focused on the tempo of battle. Counter-attack delay, enemy action intervals, auto-battle speed — adjusting just these three timing values transformed how the battles felt. It wasn't a new feature or new mechanic. Just three numbers, tuned carefully. The mood of the whole game shifted.

What makes a battle exciting isn't a formula — it's rhythm.

Cut-ins Flash — But First, a Failure

v0.2-8 ~ v0.2-8e — 2026.05.03

In Dev Log #006, I mentioned that Yi Sun-sin's unique skill cut-in image was working on the battle screen. But honestly, it wasn't finished. The image was bleeding outside the battle area, the size was covering the entire screen, and the CSS diagonal background effect was spilling everywhere.

v0.2-8 was a clean failure. The image covered the entire browser. So in v0.2-8a, I stepped back and focused on one thing first: defining the exact area where the cut-in was allowed to appear — inside the battle panel, nothing more. There's no point adjusting size when you haven't defined the container yet.

Once the boundary was set, v0.2-8b halved the image size, and v0.2-8c/d brought the CSS diagonal background effect inside the panel too. Finally, Yi Sun-sin's cut-in was flashing properly — contained, purposeful, dramatic.

Then came v0.2-8e. I expanded the cut-in system beyond Yi Sun-sin to include Nobunaga, Kenshin, and Jeong Do-jeon. Now enemy units trigger cut-ins when they act too. The flow became: unit acts → cut-in flashes → skill fires → next unit. The cut-in system accidentally fixed the battle tempo problem at the same time.

Expanding the Battlefield — 14×8 and Unit Polish

v0.2-9a ~ v0.2-9c — 2026.05.03

With the cut-in system settled, it was time to work on the battlefield itself. v0.2-9a expanded the map to 14×8 tiles. Units now had more room to maneuver, which made strategic positioning actually matter.

v0.2-9b refined the visual style of unit tokens. The flags shifted from harsh, bright red and blue to a more ink-wash aesthetic. Borders were lowered, ground shadows deepened. I wanted the battlefield to carry the weight of a strategy game, not the brightness of a mobile puzzle.

v0.2-9c improved the hero selection UI. Players can now select heroes directly from the HUD buttons on the right side. I also renamed some unique skills to better reflect their historical feel — Nobunaga's "Arquebus Fire" became Three-Stage Volley, and Kenshin's "Charge" became Wheeling Battle Formation.

Manual Combat and Enemy Invasions

v0.2-9d ~ v0.2-10 — 2026.05.05

I took May 4th off. Stepping away from the code actually helped — I came back on the 5th knowing exactly what to build next.

v0.2-9d added a manual/auto combat selection mode. Whether attacking or defending, players can now choose how to fight. But implementing manual combat surfaced a bug: when an enemy unit was in a "confused" state, it would freeze and do nothing — wasting the turn entirely.

This bug proved stubborn. ChatGPT kept patching it in parts without finding the root cause. So I brought in Claude Opus 4.7 to analyze the entire code flow from a higher level. Zooming out worked — the root cause became clear, and the fix followed quickly. Confused units now properly consume their turn.

v0.2-10 added turn exchange to the world map. After the player's turn ends, the enemy takes theirs — and may launch an invasion with some probability. When an invasion triggers, the combat selection screen appears again. If the enemy wins, the player's capital is pushed back. The world map stopped being a background and became an actual strategic board.

Making Battle Results Dramatic — Cut-in and BGM

v0.3-3a ~ v0.3-3b — 2026.05.05

Ending a battle with just a result text felt flat. v0.3-3a added a victory/defeat result image displayed at the center of the battle panel for 2 seconds — the same mechanism as the hero cut-ins. A small touch, but it makes the end of a fight feel like a moment.

The 2-second timer is designed to be replaced later: once BGM is in, the duration will match the length of the victory or defeat music. Leaving the structure open like this pays off later. v0.3-3b added those victory and defeat music tracks right away. Battle results are now a full scene, not just a popup.

EASTWAR on Mobile — Icon and Shortcut

v0.3-3c — 2026.05.06

There's something unexpectedly exciting about opening your phone and tapping an icon that launches something you built. v0.3-3c added home screen shortcut support. Full mobile optimization isn't done yet, but the game now launches directly from the phone's home screen.

The icon was generated using ChatGPT's image generation. A warship, a dragon, and the EASTWAR logo — when it appeared on my phone's home screen for the first time, it looked surprisingly legitimate.

Guan Yu and Zhang Fei Take the Field

v0.3-4a ~ v0.3-4d — 2026.05.06

EASTWAR is a game where heroes from Korea, China, and Japan clash across East Asia. But until now, only Korean (Yi Sun-sin, Jeong Do-jeon) and Japanese (Nobunaga, Kenshin) factions existed. It was finally time for China to enter the stage.

v0.3-4a laid out the data design for Guan Yu and Zhang Fei. Hero data lives in data/heroes.js, unique skills in data/skills.js. Adding a new hero isn't just dropping in an image — it means designing their stats, faction, and skill logic from scratch.

Guan Yu's unique skill is Crescent Slash: 1.6× damage to one enemy unit, plus a 25% defense boost to himself for one turn. Attack and defense in one move — very Guan Yu. Zhang Fei's unique skill is Changban Eruption: 1.5× damage to one unit, with a 40% chance to cause panic in all adjacent tiles. A pure charge-type fighter.

v0.3-4b brought in their portraits and skill images. v0.3-4c placed each faction in their home cities: Yi Sun-sin and Jeong Do-jeon in Hanyang, Nobunaga and Kenshin in Kyoto, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei in Luoyang. Three factions, three strongholds, one map.

v0.3-4d connected Pyongyang and Luoyang on the world map — two cities already linked by land that weren't connected in-game. Now the Korean and Chinese factions can directly clash. One line added to the map, and suddenly the game felt a continent wider.

A few lines of code, and a continent connected. That's what makes building a game worth it.
