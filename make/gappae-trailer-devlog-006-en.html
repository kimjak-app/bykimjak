Gappae Trailer

Production Log

How the trailer gets made, from concept and shot design to the final cut, one shot at a time.

Production Log · #006

Location Images Locked

Before moving the camera, I had to lock the space

September 26–28, 2026

Locations Had to Be Locked as Carefully as Characters

The Gappae trailer was planned for generative video models such as Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax H3. That meant locations could not remain as vague “similar-looking backgrounds.” Just like the character sheets, the spaces needed fixed visual references.

The text storyboard and illustrated storyboard already defined each beat's location, time of day, camera direction, and blocking. The next step was to turn those decisions into stable location images the video model could repeatedly reference.

Some scenes needed both front and reverse views. Others returned to the same place under different lighting. So I checked each beat again and generated only the exact spatial references the production would need.

If a character sheet is the reference value for a person, a location image is the reference value for the space the camera must move through.

Beat 1 — Seolhyangru

Used in: Beat 01 · Yeosaeng's Dance

The trailer opens on the central stage of Seolhyangru. Because the camera moves from a full-body wide shot toward close-ups, I created both a front view that clearly establishes the stage axis and a right-side view for lateral camera movement.

Beats 2–3 — Yukjo Street and the Aftermath

Used in: Beat 02 · Yi Gak's Order / Massacre · Beat 03 · Deokgu's Death

Beat 2 needed the same street to exist in two completely different states: a normal night before the order and a massacre in progress after it. Beat 3 then required a third state: the same world after the violence, with fire, smoke, broken stalls, and debris.

Beat 4 — Forest Hunting Ground

Used in: Beat 04 · Yi Gak's Arrow

Spatial direction is critical here. Yi Hyeon, the deer, and Yi Gak need to align, and the arrow has to pass the deer before reaching Yi Hyeon's side of the space. I therefore built two references for the same hunting ground: one facing Yi Gak's direction and one facing Yi Hyeon's direction.

Beat 5 — Burning Cottage

Used in: Beat 05 · Yi Hyeon's Proposal

Pilbok faces the burning house while Yi Hyeon stands behind him toward the dark yard. Their backgrounds must therefore be different within the same scene. A front view of the burning cottage and a reverse view without fire prevent the fire from accidentally jumping to the wrong side in OTS and reverse shots.

Beats 6 and 10 — Rogues' Hideout

Used in: Beat 06 · Jeokpaegwi · Beat 10 · Joining Hands

The hideout appears twice with different emotional functions. Beat 6 is rough and overcast; Beat 10 turns the same place into the setting for an alliance. I created the basic hideout, a view toward the stream, and a sunset version for the final silhouette.

Beats 7–9 — Spaces for the Trials

Used in: Beat 07 · Geumgangyacha · Beat 08 · Banggae · Beat 09 · Gaedochi / Card Montage

Each location carries the character's energy: a muddy gravesite clearing for Geumgangyacha, the open Samgaenaru river landing for Banggae, a narrow market alley for Gaedochi, then a separate lamplit gambling table for the card montage.

Beats 11–12 — Spaces for Love

Used in: Beat 11 · Pilbok × Yeosaeng Sword Practice · Beat 12 · Kiss Under Nakhwa Fireworks

Beat 11 uses a daytime cherry-blossom clearing; Beat 12 moves to a night market festival. They are different spaces and times, but they are visually connected by falling flowers: petals by day, falling sparks by night.

Beats 13–15 — Inside Seolhyangru and the Rogues' Tent

Used in: Beat 13 · Seol In-hyang · Beat 14 · Yeosaeng's Transformation · Beat 15 · Pilbok vs. Assassin

Seolhyangru's public stage is Beat 1; Beats 13 and 14 move into private rooms. I separated Seol In-hyang's room from Yeosaeng's room, then shifted to the rough, unstable texture of the rogues' tent interior for Beat 15.

Beats 16–17 — Burned Hanseong and the Outskirts Forest

Used in: Beat 16 · Pilbok Becomes a Hero · Beat 17 · Yi Hyeon Stabs Someone

Beat 16 needs public celebration against a burned residential street in Hanseong. Beat 17 removes the crowd entirely and shifts into a cold outskirts forest at night, moving from public heat into isolation.

Beat 18 — Yeongjegyo Courtyard

Used in: Beat 18 · The Road to Geunjeongjeon

This sequence is designed as a fake one-take through palace combat. A single background image is not enough. The camera must be able to turn while remaining in the same space, so I created views toward both Geunjeongmun and Heungnyemun.

Beat 19 — Inside Geunjeongjeon

Used in: Beat 19 · Yi Hyeon in Geunjeongjeon

Just before the final fight, the space has to empty out. After the crowded palace battle, Yi Hyeon sits alone inside the dark hall. The throne, red columns, and pale daylight entering through the doors all reinforce his isolation.

Beat 20 — Geunjeongjeon Front Courtyard

Used in: Beat 20 · Final Duel → Ending Poster

The final duel requires the greatest number of camera directions. Pilbok comes up from below, Yi Hyeon comes down from above, and the fight then alternates between frontal, side, and reverse views. I locked the courtyard with four references: front, reverse, east side, and west side.

Location Images Were Spatial Data, Not Background Art

The goal was never to generate as many beautiful Joseon backgrounds as possible. I followed the text storyboard and illustrated storyboard, then generated only the spaces and camera directions the production actually required.

Whenever the same location appears more than once, there is a reason: front and reverse coverage, a new time of day, or an action sequence that forces the camera to rotate through the space.

These 30 locked location images will become visual anchors when Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax H3 begin moving characters and cameras through the scenes.

Character continuity comes from character sheets. Spatial continuity comes from location references.

Characters, timing, storyboards, and locations were now ready. The next step was previs: testing whether the camera and action could actually be controlled in space.
