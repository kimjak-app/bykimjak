EASTWAR Dev Log #006

The Battle Came Alive — UI Polish and Hero Cut-ins

May 2 — May 3, 2026

In #005, the battle engine foundation was complete. Now it was time to add flesh to the bones — from a game that works to a game that feels alive.

Cleaning Up the Battle Screen

v0.2-7a — v0.2-7k

The first battle UI was honest about being a prototype. Buttons scattered, information hard to read at a glance.

One principle was set: left panel for battle log, right panel for unit info, bottom bar for commands. That role separation alone made the battle screen feel dramatically more like a game.

A series of refinements followed. Command buttons centered, direction-select UI fixed, move cancel and attack cancel mapped to right-click. Attack, skill, and stratagem range indicators added. And HP was renamed to Formation Strength — not just a health bar, but a measure of how well a unit holds its ground in battle.

One fix at a time, the screen started looking like an actual game.

Adding Feel to the Battle

v0.2-7L — v0.2-7o

Once the features were in place, a new problem appeared. Battles resolved too fast — something felt hollow.

Delays were added between enemy counterattacks, movement, and actions. Starting at 0.5 seconds, tuned up to 0.8, then settled around 1 second. Each adjustment changed the entire feel of combat. The same battle could feel tense or flat just by tweaking the tempo.

Then BGM was added — separate tracks for the world map and battle. The moment music came in, the game felt completely different.

The Heroes Burst Out — Unique Skill Cut-ins

v0.2-8 — v0.2-8e

This was the most demanding piece of work. When a hero activates their unique skill, a cut-in image bursts onto the battle screen.

The first attempt failed — the image covered the entire browser. Getting the cut-in to stay inside the battle panel was the first fix. Then came size adjustments, CSS diagonal background effect trimming, and position tuning.

Starting with Yi Sun-sin, all four heroes — Jeong Do-jeon, Nobunaga, Kenshin — received their own cut-ins. Each unique skill now triggers a distinct visual moment that transforms the screen.

A Wider Battlefield, A Living World Map

v0.2-9 — v0.2-10

The battle grid was expanded to 14×8. More space, more tactical options.

And the world map got an enemy turn. Pass the turn, and the enemy moves. They may invade — triggering a choice between auto-battle and manual combat. If they win, the allied capital gets pushed back.

The game finally became something to fight against, not just play alone.

The battle was built, polished, and the heroes took the stage. Next: putting their faces on the battlefield itself.
