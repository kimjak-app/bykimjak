Dev Log · #010

Reinforcing What You Can't See

From Working Prototype to a Structure Built to Scale

May 9 – May 12, 2026

Days Spent on Things the Screen Never Shows

This dev log reads differently from the others. No new hero, no new screen, no cut-in flashing across the battlefield. Instead, the code underneath got rebuilt. The game looks exactly the same from the outside — but the structure underneath is completely different now. Invisible to players, but decisive for every future sprint.

Keep the game loop that already works, and build a core structure sturdy enough to handle more cities, more heroes, more skills, more unit types, and naval combat down the road.

That was the goal going into May 9th.

Naming Things First — Constants and Presets

v0.5-0b — 2026.05.09

Before giving each city different domestic numbers, the first move was cleaning up constants. Core values got centralized in js/constants.js, and city-level resource and domestic presets were built on top of that foundation.

It sounds minor, but the payoff was immediate. Fewer typo bugs. Renaming something means changing it in one place. And when delegating work to an AI agent like Codex, the instruction becomes simple: "use this key." Code stability went up a notch without a single large-scale rewrite.

Wrapping, Not Replacing

v0.5-0r — 2026.05.09

The most important decision of the day: don't tear out the existing code.

window.gameState and appState, which had been managing game state, weren't removed. Instead, a new GameStore was introduced — the old state stayed exactly as it was, while every new feature going forward would route through GameStore. Wrapping, not replacing.

The event system followed the same philosophy. An EventBus was added, but not everything got turned into an event right away. Only four key moments started emitting: city selected, battle ended, city conquered, hero moved.

The save system got a skeleton too — createSaveSnapshot(), saveGame(), loadGame(), clearSave(). Built on localStorage for now, full implementation deferred.

Together, these changes gave the game a structure that could evolve safely, one step at a time.

Pulling the UI Apart

v0.5-0u — 2026.05.09

Until now, screen-rendering code and game logic had been tangled together inside main.js. This pass separated the DOM rendering code into a dedicated js/ui layer. Nothing about how the screen looks or behaves changed — only where the functions live and what each one is responsible for.

From the outside, this patch looks like nothing happened. That was exactly the point.

Becoming One, Through Modules

v0.5-0m — 2026.05.09

After re-confirming that combat, the world map, city selection, hero movement, conquest and recruitment, and the domestic scaffold were all still working correctly, the JavaScript loading system was unified under ES modules.

index.html was updated to load main.js as a module, with dependencies imported explicitly rather than relied upon implicitly. A minimal set of global exposures was kept for backward compatibility — nothing more. Phaser, however, stayed on its CDN script tag. Modularizing the game engine itself carried too much risk for too little benefit.

The principle stayed firm throughout: this was a loading-structure cleanup, not a feature addition. Combat, the world map, every existing behavior — unchanged.

Cutting the Last Bridge

v0.5-0w — 2026.05.09

The final task of May 9th: removing the transitional global exposures — window.gameState, window.GameStore, window.EventBus, and the rest — that had been left open during the migration. The game no longer carries both the old way and the new way at once.

What used to be a working prototype is now an expandable web game project.

Giving Every City a Reason to Be Taken

v0.5-1a — 2026.05.11

With the structure reinforced, work turned back to content. The resource system was finalized at eight types: rice, barley, seafood, timber, iron, horses, silk, and salt. City types were split into commercial metropolis, production-focused, and coastal trade — and the four current cities (Hanyang, Pyongyang, Luoyang, Kyoto) each got their own preset.

The scaffolding for national loyalty and city loyalty was planted here too.

This was the work of giving every city a reason worth conquering.

The Seasons Begin to Turn

v0.5-1b ~ v0.5-1c — 2026.05.11

v0.5-1b attached year and season tracking to the world turn counter. Spring, summer, autumn, winter — each now showing early, mid, and late progress. v0.5-1c made resources actually arrive at the end of each turn: seafood trickles in every turn, barley is harvested in spring, rice in autumn, and commerce income adjusts gold revenue.

The seasonal income structure designed back in Dev Log #009 started running in real code for the first time.

Taxation as a Balance Scale

v0.5-1d ~ v0.5-1d-1 — 2026.05.11

A tax slider went in — 0 to 100, defaulting to 30. Higher taxes mean more gold but falling loyalty; lower taxes mean less gold but recovering loyalty.

The formula was immediately realigned. Not every resource gets taxed the same. Food resources carry a lower rate; rare, strategic resources like salt, silk, and iron carry a higher one. Salt ended up the most strategically valuable resource of all. Commercial cities get a tax bonus on top. Taxation stopped being a single slider and became a system entangled with each city's character.

Storage, Upkeep, and the Chancellor's Policy

v0.5-1e ~ v0.5-1f-1 — 2026.05.11

The national storehouse received starting stockpiles, and hero upkeep started actually being deducted. Every 100 regular troops cost 6 rice, 5 barley, and 1 seafood per turn. Troop upkeep itself stayed in preview mode for now.

The chancellor policy system arrived the same day: balanced, agriculture-focused, commerce-focused, trade-focused, military-focused — five directions. Agriculture-focused boosts rice and barley while slightly reducing gold; commerce-focused boosts gold while slightly reducing food; military-focused reduces hero upkeep costs. Chancellor archetypes were later renamed to political, economic, administrative, diplomatic, and military-governance types.

Giving Every Hero a Political Face

v0.5-1g — 2026.05.11

Every hero introduced so far received a chancellor aptitude profile. Yi Sun-sin: military-governance 5, administrative 2. Jeong Do-jeon: political 4, administrative 3. Guan Yu: military-governance 5, political 2. Dorim: diplomatic 4, political 2. Nobunaga: economic 4, military-governance 3.

Heroes who previously existed only as fighters now had a second identity in the political arena. Yi Sun-sin commands the navy on the battlefield, but moves as a military-governance specialist in court. The foundation for one hero shifting between three roles — combatant, governor, chancellor — was completed here.

v0.5-1h added the actual chancellor appointment feature and portrait images, and cleaned up the world map's leftover temporary HUD.

Rebuilding the Screen

v0.5-2 ~ v0.5-2d — 2026.05.12

May 12th was dedicated to screen reconstruction. The old displays for public sentiment, security, agriculture, commerce, and stability were removed and rebuilt around city loyalty, governor appointment, and military status. Resources were re-sorted into food (rice, barley, seafood), strategic resources (timber, iron, horses), and specialty resources (silk, salt).

Loyalty got a color-coded display for instant readability — green above 90 for very stable, blue in the 80s for stable, yellow in the 60s and 70s for caution, red at 60 and below for danger. The "end turn" buttons for player and enemy were unified into the same visual style, and governor cards now display a portrait and type, matching the chancellor cards. Cities without a governor show "managed by chancellor delegation"; cities with one show the governor's name. Enemy invasions now trigger a large notification centered on screen — another addition from this day.

From Visible Domestic Affairs to Functioning Domestic Affairs

v0.5-3a ~ v0.5-3c — 2026.05.12

Until now, the domestic system had been numbers sitting on a panel — visible, but not actually affecting gameplay. v0.5-3a made it real. From visible domestic affairs to functioning domestic affairs.

v0.5-3b added save/load UI, supporting one slot for now.

v0.5-3c was the most delicate work of this stretch: making city loyalty actually move every turn. National loyalty was already working well through tax and chancellor effects, but city-level loyalty needed to reflect security, economic strength, and the combined influence of governor and chancellor as well.

Security got calculated from the combined troop strength of garrisoned heroes; economic strength from the city's commerce rating, population rating, gold income, and policy adjustments. One principle was set firmly in place: changes must never be drastic. Loyalty shifts per turn were capped to roughly -2 to +2, and the reasons behind each shift get summarized briefly in the UI.

Cities stopped being a panel of numbers and became something that breathes, one turn at a time.
